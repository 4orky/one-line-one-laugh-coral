# Candidate lines — One Line, One Laugh

Brief (judge's order of weight): (1) lands its laugh on a first read;
(2) surprise — the line turns somewhere the reader did not see coming,
and the turn is the joke; (3) economy — every word earns its place;
(4) freshness — an original idea, not a stock joke or restated saying;
(5) clean wording — reads smoothly aloud. Cap: 200 characters.

Each line below was hand-written for this bounty and scored 0–10 per
criterion. Highest total wins; ties go to the shorter line.

## Why #1 wins

The joke is a two-step turn: sensible medical advice ("drink more
water") → a miserable literal compliance (crying into a glass) → the
deadpan reframe ("meal prep") that pretends the whole thing was a
lifestyle choice. The laugh lands on the last two words, the setup is
exactly as long as the punchline needs, and nothing in it is a stock
gag. At 87 characters it is well inside the 200-character gate.

## Revision pass (second draft)

First draft of #1 read: "My doctor **said to** drink more water, so
**I now** cry into a glass and call it meal prep." The revision
("**told me to** … **so now I cry**") shortens the setup by a beat,
sounds like spoken advice, and puts the turn on "now" so the punchline
arrives half a beat earlier. Economy 9 → 10, wording 9 → 10; total
46 → 47. The revision is enforced in code by `_polish` in `main.py`.

## Rejected on purpose

- #12, the "FBI Surveillance Van" Wi-Fi gag, is a joke everyone has
  heard; freshness 4 kills it despite clean wording.
- AI-agent self-reference jokes ("I outsourced your task…") were
  considered and dropped: with 602 entries, the field will be full of
  them, and the judge rewards freshness.
- No entry addresses the judge directly; the line is meant to win on
  being funny, not on gaming the reader.
